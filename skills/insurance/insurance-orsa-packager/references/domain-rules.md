# Native domain method and limits

The task is an evidence-linked insurer ORSA draft. Preserve the insurer/group scope, valuation date, currency and separate available/required capital measurement bases. Materiality is a supplied, owned assessment with a rationale; this helper cannot decide whether the risk inventory is substantively complete. Every material risk must be linked to a reviewed non-baseline scenario. A scenario/horizon is unique and has a prospective projection date, owner, assumption assessment and reviewer. Baseline alone cannot cover material risk.

Every scenario/horizon requires both capital and liquidity measurements. Capital surplus = available - required; liquidity surplus = resources - outflows. Compare each computed surplus to the supplied reported surplus. Unknown, duplicated, basis-mismatched or missing measures stay unresolved, and negative surplus is an explicit review finding. Monetary tables are supplied analyzed results, not a new capital or cash-flow engine. The package does not net cross-entity capital, eliminate intercompany transactions, establish fungibility or transferability, or combine mismatched horizons. For a group scope, obtain reviewed consolidation and elimination evidence upstream and describe residual restrictions.

The reviewed profile lists required narrative sections. Retain section owners, dated evidence and substantive supplied assessments. The local governance convention requires risk, capital, liquidity and governing-body review evidence, but does not authenticate signatories or infer approval from a complete set. Open/rejected decisions remain findings. Readiness is reported through individual evidence gaps, risk links and reconciliations; there is no checklist completion percentage.

NAIC ORSA Guidance Manual December 2022 supplies context for risk-management, material-risk assessment and prospective/group solvency evidence. It highlights measurement bases, horizons, aggregation and liquidity considerations. The package does not determine filing thresholds, exemptions, domestic adoption or applicability, and does not claim that three sections or four review roles constitute legal sufficiency.
