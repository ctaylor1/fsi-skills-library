# Native domain method and limits

Annual joint-sample mode uses a complete supplied finite distribution of annual states, each with an explicit positive probability weight. Weights must sum exactly to one; no normalization or invented zero-loss mass is allowed. Zero-event states must still appear in the year inventory. Event rows are unique within a year; several components sharing an occurrence ID are first summed into one occurrence. Annual aggregate loss is the sum of occurrence losses. Annual occurrence loss is the maximum occurrence loss in that year, including zero in a no-event year.

Aggregate AAL = sum(weight × annual aggregate). Mean annual maximum occurrence loss = sum(weight × annual occurrence); keep it distinct from aggregate AAL. At threshold t, AEP = sum(weights where annual aggregate > t); OEP = sum(weights where annual occurrence > t). The strict greater-than convention is explicit and tested at equality. The supplied weights, events, model version and loss basis are retained; sampling uncertainty, secondary uncertainty and model fitness are not estimated. No interpolation to a return-period PML is performed.

Event-rate mode requires expected occurrences per year and expected additive loss per occurrence, for a complete nonduplicated catalog on one basis. AAL = sum(rate × loss) by linearity of expectation. Counts can exceed one per year. An annual probability, return period or deterministic scenario loss is not automatically a rate or expected loss. OEP/AEP remain unknown without an approved joint annual distribution. Annual aggregate covers/reinstatements can make losses nonadditive; prepare correctly modeled annual results upstream. Never sum per-peril PMLs or infer independence.

ASOP 38 provides the professional modeling context. CAS catastrophe modeling material explains the annual sum/maximum distinction. The supported formulas and exact-mass rule are explicit bounded implementation conventions, not regulatory capital calculations.
