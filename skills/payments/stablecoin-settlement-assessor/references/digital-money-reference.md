# Digital-money distinctions — reference only

Reviewed 2026-09-18; refresh due 2026-10-18. Owner: Payments treasury and product risk; instrument classification belongs to legal and accounting specialists. See [source-map.md](source-map.md) for primary evidence.

| Instrument or feature | Distinct question | Availability treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stablecoin | What are this holder's claim, issuer, reserve, redemption eligibility, custody and network exposures? | Require the actual asset, chain, provider, corridor and production-access evidence; a peg target is not guaranteed conversion. |
| Tokenized bank deposit | Which bank owes the deposit, who is eligible, and what deposit/transfer/settlement regime applies? | A token format does not erase the deposit claim or make it a payment stablecoin. Product availability is institution-specific, separately evidenced; no bundled operational planner. |
| CBDC | Which central bank liability, retail/wholesale design, eligible holders and access regime? | Distinguish research, experiment, pilot and issued production access. ECB digital euro remains a project with future pilot/potential issuance milestones on the reviewed page; do not treat it as a live alternative. |
| Programmable settlement | What conditional instructions, triggers, oracles, rollback/dispute paths and legal discharge apply? | Programming is a feature, not a legal instrument or universal rail. A technical demo does not establish legal finality, production access or autonomous payment authority. |

A source naming tokenization, atomicity or smart contracts does not settle credit, liquidity, legal or operational risk. Compare these reference concepts only when the user's question calls for them; this skill's executable options remain stablecoin versus fiat.
