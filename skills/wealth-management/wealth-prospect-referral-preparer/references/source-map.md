# Sources, permissions and applicable-rule orientation

## Source hierarchy
1. Firm data-use and consent/suppression systems: purpose, recipient/firm/channel scope, dates,
   opt-outs, current screening and the underlying evidence locator. Treat these as supplied
   records requiring real source authentication at deployment.
2. Current applicable firm policy, service catalogue and controlled disclosures: version, owner,
   dates, actual regulatory profile and specialist applicability review.
3. Prospect-provided records and expressly authorized referrals: only the permitted professional
   context, retaining reported/documented distinction and sharing-authority scope.
4. Specifically approved public records: only the authorized purpose/use. Availability on the
   internet is neither a data-use license nor prospect contact permission. No scraping or social
   enrichment is performed by this package.

Every fact and derivative question retains its source IDs. A source locator is not proof of
content, identity, permission, endorsement truth or approval. Keep incompatible observations and
permission histories visible. Do not silently replace opt-outs with a newer-looking grant.

## Official sources checked 2026-09-17
These are orientation sources for the responsible reviewers, not a complete jurisdiction pack.
No blanket applicability, registration, privacy exception or legal outcome is encoded.

- [SEC Investment Adviser Marketing guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/investment-adviser-marketing): describes Rule 206(4)-1 for SEC-registered/required-to-register advisers. Advertisement definitions, compensated endorsements/testimonials, disclosures, oversight, agreements and disqualification contain conditions and exceptions. A single invitation or referral must not be classified automatically from the existence of a referral payment.
- [SEC Marketing Compliance FAQs](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/division-investment-management-frequently-asked-questions/marketing-compliance-frequently-asked-questions): staff positions add fact-dependent nuance, including promoter-disqualification treatment. Do not copy a simplified binary eligibility decision into this workflow.
- [FINRA Rule 2040](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2040): compensation restrictions depend on the recipient's activities and registration requirements. An unregistered referrer is not automatically permitted or prohibited by this package; the appropriate reviewer must document the analysis.
- [FINRA Rule 2210](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2210): members' communication classifications and review requirements depend on communication type and audience. A one-person draft does not establish campaign-wide classification or supervisory clearance.
- [FINRA Rule 3230](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/3230): covers member telemarketing controls including firm-specific and national do-not-call restrictions, time-of-day rules and conditioned exceptions. An existing relationship does not override the member's firm-specific opt-out. Federal/state requirements may also apply. This package drafts a manual-call script only; it does not dial or select lawful calling times.
- [FTC CAN-SPAM business guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business): commercial-email requirements include sender/subject accuracy, identification, postal address and opt-out handling. Not all messages have the same legal classification. The package's affirmative-permission-only restriction is deliberately narrower than an assertion of universal opt-in law.
- [SEC Regulation S-P privacy release](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2000/06/privacy-consumer-financial-information-regulation-s-p): provides privacy-notice and nonaffiliated-sharing context, including relevant exceptions. Marketing contact permission is different from authority to disclose a consumer's financial information.
- [SEC 2024 Regulation S-P amendments](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2024/06/s7-05-23): address safeguards, incident response, notification and related records/privacy provisions for covered institutions. [SEC privacy/identity-theft resources](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-trading-markets/broker-dealers/sec-rules-relating-privacy-identity-theft) also identify affiliate-marketing rules. Privacy owners must assess current federal/state and capacity-specific obligations; this module does not decide coverage or exemptions.

The synthetic fixture policy, dates and reviewer records are test data, never legal guidance or
production approvals. Revalidate source versions and firm/state requirements before controlled use.
