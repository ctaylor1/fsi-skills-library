# Bounded practitioner method

Compare the exact information set, classification, employee capacity, business purpose, need-to-know and requested period with the supplied authority mandate. Assess proposed restrictions and watch/restricted-list changes separately; a permission for one information set, purpose, actor or period never transfers to another. Unknown materiality/nonpublic status requires specialist review, not public classification. Identify mismatches, missing controls and required control-room decisions.

## Supported scope

The native method reconciles supplied facts using the schema; it does not contain a complete
legal-rule engine. Institutional review criteria must carry exact source-fact references,
version, scope and dates. No legal profile, legal threshold, risk weight, materiality decision
or authority is installed by default. Record unsupported jurisdiction/product/sector profiles
and route contested applicability to Legal. Human narrative must separately distinguish
verified fact, supplied assertion, inference, recommendation and unknown.

## Control-room review detail

Reconcile the actual proposed transition before recommending a review disposition: identify
originating team, intended recipient/capacity, issuer/instruments or information objects,
information classification and reason for sharing. Obtain the exact written mandate, need-to-know
basis and start/end dates. Inspect list changes individually rather than interpreting access
permission as a watch/restricted-list waiver. Distinguish creating an entry, removing an entry,
and changing its scope; use exact proposed changes in the supplied fact list.

The calculator compares supplied authority fields and does not determine legal materiality,
when information became public, restriction-release conditions or instrument equivalence.
The human draft must identify those decisions, contrary classification evidence, controls to
apply while a decision is open, and the control-room owner. Any changed person, purpose,
information set, authority or period requires a new input and review. Broad executive access
and seniority do not establish need-to-know or permission.
