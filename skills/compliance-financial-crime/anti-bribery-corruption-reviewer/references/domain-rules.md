# Bounded practitioner method

Reconcile the service agreement, delivered work and payment/benefit recipient. Calculate supplied compensation-to-benchmark ratio only when amounts and currency are comparable, then compare to an explicit institution limit. Review ownership/intermediary due diligence and actual government links; a government link is a review trigger, not proof of corruption. Retain counterevidence and contested facts. Unknown policy, benchmark, recipient, services or diligence prevents a favorable disposition.

## Supported scope

The native method reconciles supplied facts using the schema; it does not contain a complete
legal-rule engine. Institutional review criteria must carry exact source-fact references,
version, scope and dates. No legal profile, legal threshold, risk weight, materiality decision
or authority is installed by default. Record unsupported jurisdiction/product/sector profiles
and route contested applicability to Legal. Human narrative must separately distinguish
verified fact, supplied assertion, inference, recommendation and unknown.

## Benefit and intermediary review detail

Build the draft around the actual engagement/benefit: service specification, legitimate business
purpose, recipient and ultimate beneficiary, payer/payment destination, timing, value and relevant
policy version. Compare contract description with performed work and invoices. Obtain the
institution's comparable benchmark and currency; the native ratio is amount / benchmark amount,
rounded to 12 decimal places. A zero benchmark or currency mismatch withholds the ratio. The
supplied limit is institutional evidence, never a legal safe harbor.

The professional draft examines intermediary ownership/qualifications, diligence red flags,
official connections, unusual terms, payment routing and plausible counterexplanations. The
native completeness/government-link flags reflect supplied reviewer facts and do not replace
that examination. Retain counterevidence prominently; a flagged relationship or high ratio is
not criminal intent or guilt. Route a payment approval to the governed payment/procurement
owner and a suspected misconduct allegation to an independently authorized investigation.
