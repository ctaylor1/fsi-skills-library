# Method and boundaries

AM owns service accountability and transition-control review. The pinned operations-performance-analyzer owns queue conservation, completed-cohort times, cost and SLA calculations. Its arithmetic and limitations remain unchanged; a separate native output is reconstructed from the detached input, not trusted because a hash matches. Shared unresolved issues carry into the AM result.

A service baseline is comparable only when population and definition version match the shared observed cohort. No service aggregation, annualization, wage saving, automation capacity, FTE recommendation or causal improvement is calculated. Distinct funds/books should use distinct reviews when their units or operating calendars differ.

Each declared service requires source-bound ownership, a control and a transition record. Dependencies must reference known services and be acyclic. Maker and checker must differ. An observed pass must match the specified control version; actual dates cannot exceed the review, knowledge or source-known date. Open exceptions, missing rights or unmatched populations block ready_for_owner_review. Topological order is proposed sequencing only, not implementation authorization.

The bounded native inventory cannot determine whether every legally or operationally necessary function was supplied. The practitioner must reconcile the service inventory against the actual manager's fund, book and provider registers and applicable reviewed obligations. Do not infer legal duties or deadlines from a jurisdiction name.
